How to Choose an Online Personal Trainer

By the FitnessThyme editorial team · Published November 2, 2026

Disclosure: this page contains no affiliate links today. If we add any, they will be labeled and may earn FitnessThyme a commission. Read more.

How this guide was made. It is based on the published sources listed at the bottom. The FitnessThyme editorial team has not personally tested these services, and prices and plans change, so confirm current details with each provider before you pay.

Online personal training ranges from a human coach who rewrites your program every week to an app that serves pre-built classes. Both get called “coaching,” so the first job is to know what you are actually buying. Use the criteria below to compare any option, including ones we don’t mention.

1. Decide what level of support you need

Ask yourself honestly how much accountability you need. If you stay consistent on your own and want a good plan, a self-guided app can be enough. If you tend to drift without someone checking in, a human coach who messages you first may matter more than any feature list.

2. Check the coach’s credentials

One published buyer’s guide suggests looking for a nationally accredited certification (it names NASM-CPT as an example), a relevant degree, and several years of experience.[1] That guide is written by a company that sells coaching, so treat it as one reasonable checklist rather than a standard. Ask any provider what its hiring requirements are for the whole roster, not just the featured coaches.

3. Look for real customization

A program built around your schedule, equipment, injuries and goals is different from a template you pick from a library. Ask whether the coach changes your plan based on how your workouts actually went, or only at scheduled check-ins.

4. Understand how you will communicate

Find out whether you talk to your coach directly or to a support team, and through which channels (text, video, calls). Fortune’s reviewer noted that one human-coach service communicated by message or FaceTime but had no live chat in its app, which is the kind of detail that is easy to miss until you are a customer.[2]

5. Get the full price in writing

Ask for the monthly cost, the billing period (some services bill several months at once), the cancellation process, and whether any introductory price changes after the first month. Our cost guide shows the published ranges.

6. Make sure you can switch coaches

Chemistry matters. Ask whether you can change coaches and whether there is a penalty for doing so.

7. Use the trial on purpose

If there is a trial, treat it as a test: send a question on day one and see how quickly and substantively the coach answers. Check whether the coach reaches out first or waits for you. Note any trial that requires payment details, and set a reminder to cancel if it isn’t right.

Quick scorecard

  • I know whether I want a human coach, an AI-built plan, or classes.
  • I know the coach’s qualifications and the company’s hiring standards.
  • My plan will be built for my schedule, equipment and limitations.
  • I know who I will talk to and how.
  • I have the total price, billing period and cancellation steps.
  • I can switch coaches.
  • I have tested response time during a trial.

If you have an injury, a medical condition, or are returning after a long break, talk to your doctor before starting a new program, and tell your coach.
